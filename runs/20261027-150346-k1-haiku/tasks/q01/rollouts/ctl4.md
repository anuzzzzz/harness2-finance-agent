### Final answer
**Short answer (as of April 7, 2025):** U.S. Steel has treated the Nippon Steel merger as still alive but blocked. It has pursued litigation against the government, defended the deal publicly, and sought to keep operations and labor commitments stable while the deal's future was uncertain. Its main operational warnings were that, without Nippon's capital, it would shift production and possibly move its headquarters. Note: some search results describe events after April 7, 2025 (such as the June 2025 closing and the later withdrawal of the activist campaign). I excluded those because they postdate the question's date.

**1. The regulatory block and the company's legal response**
- The government stopped the deal. <cite index="12-4">President Joe Biden blocked the $14.9bn sale of the Pittsburgh-based US Steel to Japan's Nippon Steel</cite> on January 3, 2025. The order also required the parties to <cite index="15-12">fully and permanently abandon the proposed acquisition within 30 days</cite>, with <cite index="15-14">weekly certifications</cite> to CFIUS until abandonment was complete.
- U.S. Steel and Nippon chose to fight rather than abandon. <cite index="13-19">On January 6, 2025, the first business day after Biden's decision, both Nippon Steel and U.S. Steel filed separate lawsuits</cite> challenging the order. The companies argued that <cite index="14-6">election-season political considerations undermined formal government review of the merger.</cite>
- A second suit targeted rivals. <cite index="15-24,15-25">U.S. Steel and Nippon Steel filed a second lawsuit against Cleveland-Cliffs, a rival steelmaker, and the USW, accusing them of colluding to block the acquisition.</cite>
- The timeline was extended. <cite index="10-12">On January 12, the Biden administration announced that they had delayed the executive order to block the merger back to June 18, 2025, giving courts time to review the lawsuit.</cite>

**2. Messaging on operations and commitments**
- The companies framed the deal as necessary for the business. <cite index="12-11">Nippon Steel and U.S. Steel had characterised the merger as a lifeline for the flagging US company</cite>.
- The company warned of operational consequences if the deal failed. Per Al Jazeera, <cite index="11-3">US Steel has warned that without Nippon Steel's cash, it will move production to cheaper non-union furnaces and move its Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania headquarters to another place</cite>.
- Nippon's commitments to workers were central to the pitch. <cite index="12-15">Nippon Steel's attempts to calm concerns by pledging a pause on any layoffs or closures of unionised facilities through the current union contract expiring in September 2026</cite> did not win wider acceptance. <cite index="11-10">The Japanese firm offered to move its US headquarters to Pittsburgh and promised to honour all agreements in place between US Steel and the USW.</cite>
- Financial exposure: <cite index="12-12">Nippon had paid a hefty premium to make the purchase and faces a $565m penalty payment to US Steel following the deal's collapse.</cite>
- The company also cited investment plans. It said the deal would bring <cite index="17-10">substantial investments into American facilities, including $1 billion for the Mon Valley Works plant.</cite>

**3. Governance and shareholder response**
- An activist challenged the deal. <cite index="10-13">In January 2025, activist investment company Ancora Holdings launched a proxy battle against U.S. Steel, which would seek to end the Nippon Steel merger, replace Burritt as CEO, and pursue its own strategy</cite>.
- The board defended the merger. It <cite index="17-9">maintained that the merger with Nippon Steel is the optimal route for U.S. Steel, its workforce, and the U.S. steel sector.</cite> The board <cite index="18-2">unanimously recommends that U. S. Steel stockholders vote on the WHITE proxy card</cite> for its own nominees, and the <cite index="18-3">2025 Annual Meeting will be held on May 6, 2025.</cite>
- Ancora asked for a delay. It argued the board <cite index="23-5">can and should delay the Annual Meeting until after the June 18 outside date within the merger agreement with Nippon</cite>, citing the lack of deal and litigation detail in the proxy.
- Disclosure was adjusted under SEC review. In response to a staff comment, the company <cite index="21-1">proposed to add to the proxy statement language relating to the current status of the Nippon Steel transaction and the related litigation and the Board's considerations.</cite>

**Bottom line:** As of April 7, 2025, the company's strategy was to litigate the block, keep the Nippon deal and its labor and investment pledges in front of stakeholders, and warn that failure would force changes to production and its headquarters. Its board was defending the deal against an activist proxy campaign ahead of the May 6, 2025 annual meeting.
