### Final answer
**Short answer (as of April 7, 2025):** U.S. Steel has treated the Nippon Steel deal as still pending, not closed. It is contesting the January 2025 presidential block in court, has agreed to operate under restrictions in the merger agreement, and has kept running its own business and investment plans in the meantime. Its disclosures say the deal's effects on employees, customers and suppliers are uncertain.

**1. Deal status and legal path**
- The merger agreement was signed December 18, 2023, and stockholders approved it April 12, 2024. <cite index="4-4">On January 3, 2025, President Biden issued an order prohibiting the Transaction and requiring the parties to abandon the Merger Agreement within thirty days.</cite> <cite index="4-5">On January 10, 2025, CFIUS granted an extension of that deadline to June 18, 2025.</cite>
- <cite index="4-7">On January 6, 2025, the Company, NSC and Purchaser jointly filed a lawsuit in the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit alleging that President Biden's order blocking the Transaction was issued for purely political reasons</cite>. The 10-K says the petition asks the court to set aside the CFIUS process and the order and to order a new review. The company also states there is <cite index="4-6">no assurance</cite> of success; that is, the lawsuit "is not assured to succeed" (10-K risk factors).
- <cite index="4-6">On April 6, 2025, the President of the United States issued a Presidential Memorandum directing CFIUS to conduct a de novo review of the Transaction and to submit a recommendation to the President of the United States by May 21, 2025.</cite>
- The company's March 2025 proxy correspondence with the SEC shows it was adjusting disclosure to reflect that the transaction's <cite index="1-5">"pending merger with Nippon Steel"</cite> language needed to reflect its current status and the board's considerations, and the company said it would update for later developments.

**2. Operating restrictions under the merger agreement (per the FY2024 10-K, filed January 31, 2025)**
- The company says the agreement <cite index="4-2">requires us to operate in the ordinary course of business</cite> and restricts certain actions without Nippon's consent. The filing warns these limits may prevent it from pursuing otherwise attractive opportunities.
- Acquisitions, divestitures and consolidations need Nippon's consent, as do additional debt incurrence and additional share repurchases. The company said it does not expect to use the rest of its buyback authorization for this reason.
- The 10-K says the agreement also includes non-solicitation provisions that eliminate its ability to consider alternative transaction proposals. If the deal ends, it may be unable to find a comparable transaction.

**3. Effects on employees, customers and suppliers**
- The 10-K says uncertainty about the merger's effect on employees may hurt the business, and that retaining key personnel may be harder while the deal is pending.
- It says it cannot predict how customers, distributors, suppliers and partners will react, and it aims to reassure them to keep doing business. It also says a failed deal could create negative reactions from employees and strain relations with the United Steelworkers (USW).
- About 80% of employees in North America and Slovakia are covered by collective bargaining agreements, and the USW agreements expire September 1, 2026. The 10-K does not describe any merger-linked retention bonus or workforce commitment.
- Wheeling-Nippon Steel, a Nippon subsidiary, is a customer: net sales to it were $322 million in 2024.

**4. How it is running the business meanwhile**
- Capital plans continue. The company says it is focused on deploying capital into its "Best for All" strategy, BR2 construction continued through 2024 with first coil in October 2024, and 2025 capex is expected to be about $1.0 billion, significantly lower than 2024 because several strategic projects are finished.
- Management time is a stated cost: integration planning requires substantial management commitments, which could divert attention from operations.
- If the merger fails, the company warns it would lose the benefits of Nippon's commitments, including at least $2.7 billion of capital investment and technology sharing.

**Limitations:** The 10-K does not disclose the termination fee or outside-date terms; those are in the merger agreement exhibit filed with the December 2023 8-K. I did not find or use post-April 7, 2025 developments.
